Recording Script

Every line the games say: 583 lines across 8 games, the sticker book and the play timer. 91 of them are marked ★ first: record those first.

Who you are

You’re the friendly grown-up voice of the games: think of a warm, playful children’s TV presenter talking to one 3- or 4-year-old sitting right next to you. Smile while you talk (it can be heard). Be clear and a little slower than normal, but never babyish or sing-song. A few lines belong to characters (a hungry caterpillar, animal customers, Pip the penguin): the “Who” column says so. A small change of voice is plenty; you don’t need to do impressions.

How to record

  • One file per line, named with the line’s number: 001.m4a, 002.m4a and so on. Any format is fine: a phone’s voice memos (.m4a), .wav, .mp3.
  • A quiet room with soft things around (a bedroom with curtains and a duvet is better than a kitchen). Turn off fans and the TV.
  • Phone about a hand’s width from your mouth, a little to the side so your breath doesn’t pop. Keep the same distance and loudness for every line.
  • Leave a moment of silence before and after each line. The silence is trimmed off and the loudness is evened out afterwards.
  • Say each line exactly as written. Lines that end with “!” are excited; “?” goes up at the end; “…” trails off.
  • Leave out the child’s name. Some lines (like “Well done!”) say the child’s name in the built-in voice. A recording plays for every child, so record them just as written here. (Recording in a game’s grown-up settings on your own device is the exception: there you can say the name.)
  • Mistakes are fine: just record that line again and keep the better take.
  • Short on time? Record the ★ lines first: they’re heard the most, or the built-in voice can’t say them well (like the letter sounds).

Quicker with a laptop

Recording 583 separate phone files takes a while. With Audacity (free), record one long take reading the lines in order with a pause between them. Then put a label at the start of each line (Ctrl+B, or ⌘B on a Mac) and type its number as the label. File → Export → Export Multiple, split by labels and named by label, makes all the numbered files in one go.

When you’re done

The numbered files go in the games’ voice/recorded/ folder (on GitHub: open the folder, then Add file → Upload files). The games then use your voice for every line you’ve recorded, and the AI voice for the rest. You can do it in batches. Each one just adds to or replaces what’s there.

You can also record lines one at a time on a device, in any game’s grown-up settings (hold the gear for 3 seconds). Those recordings stay on that device only.
